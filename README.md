# Subcultural-Analysis
Subcultural Identity and Willingness to Spend at Anime Conventions Evidence from Survey Data
Anime conventions and related cultural consumption have become an important part of the youth subcultural economy. Using survey data and linear regression models, this study examines whether subcultural identity increases participants’ willingness to spend on future anime conventions, merchandise, and related activities. The results show a significant positive relationship between subcultural identity and future willingness to spend. This finding remains robust when an ordered logit model is used, a more strictly screened sample is retained, and influential observations are excluded. The heterogeneity analysis indicates that this relationship is concentrated among adults, participants with stronger external support, and those with a longer history of engagement with the subculture. The mechanism analysis further suggests that subcultural identity affects future spending by significantly strengthening impulsive buying tendencies and future behavioral intentions. These findings demonstrate that subcultural identity is not merely a form of emotional attachment but may also translate into sustained demand for cultural consumption.
